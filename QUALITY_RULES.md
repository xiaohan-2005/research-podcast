# Research Podcast Quality Rules

## 1. Evidence before eloquence
- Every substantive factual claim in the dialogue must be supported by one or more source IDs.
- Do not invent sample sizes, effect sizes, dates, methods, conclusions, citations, or author intent.
- When the source is uncertain, say so in the dialogue or omit the claim.

## 2. Separate source facts from host interpretation
- `fact`: directly supported by source material.
- `interpretation`: a reasonable inference, clearly framed as interpretation.
- `question`: a host question; may be uncited if it does not assert a fact.
- `transition`: conversational glue; should not introduce new factual claims.

## 3. Natural audio, not a paper read aloud
- Use short turns and real reactions.
- Explain jargon at first use.
- Prefer examples, contrasts, and intuitive analogies over dense definitions.
- Do not vocalize citation IDs unless the user explicitly requests spoken citations.

## 4. Research integrity
- Distinguish correlation from causation.
- Preserve limitations and uncertainty.
- Do not turn a null result into evidence of no effect.
- Do not upgrade exploratory analysis into confirmatory evidence.

## 5. Final QA
Before audio generation, verify:
- all source IDs exist;
- no unsupported factual turns;
- speaker labels are valid;
- no empty text;
- target duration is plausible;
- the opening states the topic, not fabricated credentials or affiliations.
